How these are calculated
  • Timetable figures use the regular weekday timetable. The base route of each direction is its most frequent variant; other variants are longer or shorter than it, or depot runs.
  • Scheduled average speed: distance divided by travel time of the base route's slowest and fastest scheduled run. Time at stops counts.
  • Standard end stops are where buses turn round in service: a brigade ends a run there and starts its next one within 30 minutes. Depot runs start or end away from them, as a brigade's first or last run or next to a break of 30 minutes or more. A variant is shown as a depot run when most of its trips are. A bus waiting longer at a standard end stop is on a break, not at the depot.
  • Nearby end stops: end stops less than 500 m apart, such as the arrival and departure platforms of a terminus, are shown as one, named after the stop where runs end. Every stop of such a group is within 500 m of all the others. This is a rule of thumb: a depot run's last stop can be close to a terminus (e.g. Branickiego/Świętojańska, 480 m from Piastowska/Pętla on line 103) and is still a depot stop, because no run turns round there. Depot-run stops are grouped the same way.
  • Zone 2: stops in fare zone 2 (outside Białystok). The zone border isn't in the timetable, so the stretch between the last city stop and the first zone-2 stop counts half.
  • Brigades come from the trip ids. The times at the row ends are the first departure and last arrival in the timetable; the drive between the depot and that stop isn't in it.
  • Full runs are runs of the base route or a longer variant recorded from the first stop to the terminus, on weekdays only, charted per route pattern (both directions together). Positions arrive about once a minute with the next stop, so the bus passed a stop between two reports: the midpoint is used (about ±30 s for a single run, rounded to minutes). Runs more than twice as long or half as short as the timetable are left out as faulty.
  • Average delay per 15 minutes, from every position report of the line on weekdays, except buses waiting at the first stop and reports over 30 minutes late (likely errors). Negative means early.
  • Vehicles come from every recorded day, weekends too. A bus seen for fewer than 5 reports on a brigade in a day is left out as a feed glitch, so it doesn't count as a vehicle change.
  • Speed between stops: total distance over total time of all peak-hour full runs on that stretch, so the timing error of single reports averages out. It includes time spent at the stop.
