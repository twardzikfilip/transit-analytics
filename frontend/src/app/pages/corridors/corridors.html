How these are calculated
  • Corridor: from the regular weekday timetable, in three steps.
    1. For each set of 2 or more lines, the longest stretch of at least 4 consecutive stops they all run along.
    2. A shorter one lying inside a longer one is dropped unless it carries at least 1.5 times as many buses (all of its stops), e.g. where an extra line joins for a few stops.
    3. The rest are joined end to end where one ends on the stops the next starts with and the way on doesn't fork (all ways on lead to the same next stop). On a joined corridor, lines can join or leave partway: lines running all of it have a filled badge, lines running at least half of it an outlined one.
    The stops, start and end shown are those of the busier direction.
  • Both directions are one row when at least half of the lines run back between the same ends (stops with the same name, or less than 500 m apart) or along at least half of the same stops. Otherwise the row is marked "one way": in the centre, many lines go back along other streets.
  • Buses a day, interval and regularity are measured on each stretch between two stops (every bus using it) and averaged along the corridor, weighted by the stretches' length, so a line running part of it counts for that part. Buses: both directions added up; the rest averaged over the directions.
  • Interval: on each stretch, the minutes of the period divided by the buses leaving its first stop in it.
  • Regularity: how evenly those buses are spread, as the coefficient of variation (CV) of the gaps between them: standard deviation ÷ mean, on each stretch per period window (needs 4 buses), weighted by the number of gaps. 0 means perfectly even. Lines sharing a corridor are rarely timed together, so corridors are usually less regular than single lines. Grades as in TCQSM: {{ gradeBands }}
