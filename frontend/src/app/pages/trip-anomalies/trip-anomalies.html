How trips are judged
  • Every trip of the timetable scheduled to run in the window (for any part of it) is matched to the recorded positions: a report belongs to the trip of its brigade and line scheduled to leave its next stop closest to the report's time minus its delay (the headsign helps, but the feed's don't always read as the timetable's).
  • Seen is the stretch of the trip the bus reported heading to. A trip counts as run in full when first seen heading to one of its first 3 stops and last to one of its last 2 (reports come every 10 seconds).

  • Only a bus reporting all along (reports at most 90 s apart) shows that it short-turned; when its reports stop and it comes back for its brigade's next trip, it's a gap in tracking (no signal). A detour is 3 reports in a row more than 150 m from the trip's route shape, between its first and last stops. A bus change needs both buses to report at least 3 times on the trip under its brigade, one after the other, the first past the trip's first 3 stops and the second going further than the first got. A change on a trip run only partly is described in its note. The replaced bus is possibly broken down when it isn't used again that day: it doesn't move 500 m away and keep reporting a brigade for 10 minutes (a broken bus keeps reporting where it stands, sometimes under its brigade's other trips); otherwise it was reassigned to another line or brigade, or came back to the same brigade later.
  • A trip is judged as soon as it's clear what the bus did; until then it's under way. Trips during a gap in recording (3 minutes or more without any report) aren't judged. A trip not seen 10 minutes after it was due is listed as missing "so far" until 15 minutes after its scheduled arrival; a bus is lost after 15 minutes without reports. Only lines in the city timetable count.
