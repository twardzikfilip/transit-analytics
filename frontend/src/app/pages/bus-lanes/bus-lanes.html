How this is measured
  • Bus lanes are every street section OpenStreetMap tags with a lane for buses (busway, bus:lanes, psv:lanes, lanes:bus), fetched again every month. Length counts each direction's lane. Bus bays and terminus loops aren't lanes and are left out.
  • On a bus lane: each pair of consecutive position reports of a bus (5–90 s apart) counts where its middle is: on a lane when within 15 m of one and heading its way (within 45°). Within 30 m of a lane running the opposite way, it is on the same street the other way.
  • Speed is the distance driven over the time taken, stops and waits included, as passengers ride it. Layovers at termini, gaps over 90 s and GPS jumps are left out; only the timetable's lines inside the city count.
  • Delay change is how the delay the feed reports changed between the two reports, within one trip, per km driven: positive means buses fell further behind the timetable there, negative that they made up time (timetables allow for traffic, so buses often make up time on open roads).
  • Reading it: bus lanes are built on the most congested streets, through the centre with many stops and lights, so they are slower than the city's average however well they work. The fairer comparisons are the same street the other way without a lane, and how much slower each is at peak than off-peak: a lane that works keeps its peak speed.
