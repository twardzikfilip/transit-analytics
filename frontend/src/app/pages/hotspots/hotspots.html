How hotspots are found
  • Standstill: a bus on a line reporting twice or more in a row (every 10 seconds) within 10 m. Its time counts from the first of those reports to the last, so waits shorter than about 10 seconds are mostly missed.
  • Away from stops: standstills within 50 m of a stop are boarding, and within 150 m of a terminal are layovers; both are left out, and so are standstills longer than 5 minutes (breaks, breakdowns). Only lines in the city timetable count, since other operators' stops aren't known.
  • Hotspots: standstills are grouped where they gather (DBSCAN: standstills within 25 m of each other, at least 5 together) and a group needs at least 3 different buses. Time lost is the standing time altogether; slowing down and pulling away aren't included, so the real loss is larger.
  • Heading is the direction the buses faced while waiting, so a junction shows which approach holds them up. Busiest hour is the hour of the day with the most time lost.
