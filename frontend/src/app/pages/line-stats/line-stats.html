How these are calculated
  • Length: the main route of each direction (the variant most trips use), measured along the route's shape; the two directions are averaged.
  • Travel time: first departure to last arrival of trips on the main route. Short turns and depot runs are left out.
  • Avg speed: total distance divided by total travel time of main-route trips that start in the period, so longer trips weigh more. It includes time spent at stops.
  • Interval: the minutes in the period divided by the departures in it, per direction, then averaged over the directions. "—" means no departures then.
  • Regularity: how evenly departures are spread, as the coefficient of variation (CV) of the gaps between consecutive departures: standard deviation ÷ mean. It is measured at the first stop of the main route, counting every trip of the line that departs there, per direction and period (the break between the two peaks is not a gap), then averaged weighted by the number of gaps. 0 means perfectly even. Grades follow TCQSM: A ≤ 0.21, B ≤ 0.30, C ≤ 0.39, D ≤ 0.52, E ≤ 0.74, F above. For passengers arriving at random the average wait is half the mean gap × (1 + CV²), so uneven spacing adds waiting even at the same frequency. "—" means fewer than 4 departures in the period.
  • Vehicles: buses (vehicle duties) with a trip running in the period. Each period has two windows (morning and afternoon peak; late morning and evening off-peak): the busier of the two counts.
  • All figures are planned (from the timetable), for a regular weekday.
